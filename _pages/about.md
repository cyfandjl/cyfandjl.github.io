I am a Ph.D. candidate in Nuclear Engineering and Radiological Sciences at the **University of Michigan**, advised by [Prof. Peng Zhang](https://ners.engin.umich.edu/people/zhang-peng/). My research area is **Plasmas and Nuclear Fusion**, and I expect to graduate in **summer 2027**.

My research connects quantum electron emission, computational plasma physics, and scientific machine learning. I study ultrafast photoemission, circuit-coupled gas discharge, and plasma formation and startup.

## Research

- **Ultrafast electron emission.** Exact quantum and time-dependent Schrödinger-equation models of laser-driven photoemission, including energy-resolved spectra, quantum efficiency, pulse-shape effects, and carrier-envelope-phase dependence.
- **Gas discharge and surface emission.** Co-author of **PASCHEN-1D**, a one-dimensional fluid plasma solver with multiple surface-emission mechanisms and flexible external-circuit coupling.
- **Fusion startup.** Preionized-plasma modeling within the **University of Michigan–Helion HERCULES collaboration**, studying early ionization, plasma formation, and edge behavior.
- **Scientific machine learning.** Surrogate and interpretable models for physical simulations; co-authored 2026 atmospheric-science work on turbulence-dissipation reconstruction and Doppler-lidar mixing-layer-height retrieval.

## Nuclear AI research

From **June 22 to August 29, 2026**, I worked with **Prof. Aditi Verma** at the University of Michigan on AI in nuclear safety, security, and safeguards, including governance, regulation, and historical development. I contributed to survey design for a nuclear-sector AI observatory.

## Background

Before joining Michigan, I earned an M.S. in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Michigan State University, an M.S. in Physical Electronics and a B.S. in Fundamental Science of Mathematics and Physics at Yunnan University. I also trained at the Chinese Academy of Sciences Institute of Physics and worked as a TFT-LCD mask-design engineer at Chuzhou HKC.
